Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, "Validation and Falsification of a
Flow-Coherent Directed-Field Model for Reversible-Lane Initialization," for
publication as a Research Article in *Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory*.

The manuscript presents a lane-conserving directed-field model and a
reproducible verification and validation workflow for reversible-lane candidate
generation under uncertain demand. The model is implemented with JAX
derivatives and is evaluated through exact dynamic-programming verification,
paired synthetic experiments, a public-network transfer test, and an external
observed-count audit. The paper reports negative evidence as a primary result:
a strong downstream solver removes final-quality differences and D-SFTF does not
provide general convergence acceleration. It also identifies when direct proxy
plans improve and why topology-only smoothing and uncalibrated public demand can
fail.

We believe the work fits SIMPAT because it makes the model-development,
implementation, scalability, verification, validation, and falsification steps
explicit and reproducible. Its contribution is not a claim of field-deployed
traffic control or replacement of traffic assignment. Instead, it demonstrates
a bounded modelling component and an evidence hierarchy that prevents proxy
quality from being mistaken for operational validity.
